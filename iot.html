Routing Firebase & RFID Final hands-on project

How the car decides where to go and talks to the cloud. Routing covers graphs and Dijkstra's shortest path, multitasking firmware under FreeRTOS, and line tracking; Firebase & RFID covers a realtime cloud database and touchless identification. The pathway ends with a hands-on project that connects the cars to one shared dashboard. Further units are planned.

Units

What is in this pathway

Routing

Graphs, Dijkstra, and FreeRTOS multitasking

Every navigation app solves the same problem: the cheapest route on a weighted graph. Students run Dijkstra's algorithm in MATLAB, then put it on the car with multitasking firmware, infrared line tracking, and decisions at a crossroad.

What it covers
  • Graphs & Dijkstra: nodes, edges, and weights; a worked example and a Hong Kong traffic map; simulating the algorithm in MATLAB.
  • Multitasking: FreeRTOS tasks, priorities, and pinning tasks to CPU cores; why a single superloop stops being enough.
  • Line tracking: three IR sensors reading black vs. white surfaces; planning behaviour with a truth table; movement as a finite-state machine, including the crossroad decision.

Firebase & RFID

Cloud database and touchless identification

Connect the car to the cloud and locate it on the playfield. A Firebase Realtime Database lets an application and the car act on the same data within seconds, and RFID tags along the road (the physics of the Octopus card) tell the car where it is.

What it covers
  • Internet architecture: the client-server model, IP addresses, ports, and DNS; JSON as the data format; HTTP requests and lightweight MQTT publish/subscribe.
  • Shared data streams: publishing live telemetry, real-time dashboards, cloud-to-device (OTA) commands, and the safety side of remote control.
  • RFID localization: passive tags embedded in the playfield as landmarks; a reader under the car resolves its position to a node.
Slides Student guide Deliverable · live read/write loop demo
Students will be able to
  • Explain the client-server model and choose JSON / HTTP / MQTT appropriately
  • Program multitasking firmware with FreeRTOS tasks and priorities
  • Publish live telemetry to a cloud database and render it on a dashboard
  • Receive and safely act on remote commands and OTA parameter changes
  • Use an RFID array as a localization layer, and state its limits
Final hands-on project

The fleet dashboard. Every car publishes status and RFID-resolved position to a shared cloud, and one dashboard shows the whole fleet live; a remote operator can start, stop, or retask any car in seconds. Materials in preparation.
